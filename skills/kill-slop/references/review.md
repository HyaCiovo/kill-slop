# Output and verification

## 1. Audit output

Report in priority order:

1. Blockers: unclear positioning, misleading evidence, broken CTAs, unusable mobile interactions, and similar failures.
2. High-priority issues: repeated content, excessive reading, unclear conversion paths, and related problems.
3. **Evidence gaps**: claims lacking proof, recommended materials, and verification methods.
4. Visual and motion recommendations.
5. Strengths worth retaining.
6. Actual verification performed and any unverified scope.

Point to specific sections or files and explain each recommendation. Do not assign a mechanical score or trigger a rewrite based on the number of findings.

## 2. Build or improvement output

Include only what the task needs:

1. Page type, audience, use case, and primary conversion action.
2. Core message, hero copy, and CTA.
3. Page structure and each section's distinct job.
4. Evidence inventory and gaps.
5. Content layering and mobile strategy.
6. Reasons for motion and decorative media choices.
7. SEO, interaction, performance, and delivery requirements.
8. Actual verification results.

Do not produce a strategy document merely to make the format look complete.

## 3. Self-review checklist

This checklist is for the agent before delivery. It is not a user-facing scorecard and must not mechanically trigger a rewrite.

### Information and evidence

- Can visitors quickly understand the product, audience, and use case?
- Is there one clear primary CTA?
- Does every important claim have nearby supporting evidence?
- Have evidence gaps been listed separately?
- Are numbers, cases, third-party validation, demos, and real outcomes clearly distinguished?
- Are any sections repetitive, vague, or lacking a distinct job?

### Visuals and content

- Are typography, sizing, spacing, radii, and colors consistent?
- Does every section use the same card template?
- Does decoration or motion compete with the core message?
- Do headings and body copy wrap naturally and scan easily?

### Mobile and interaction

- Is mobile merely a vertical stack of desktop content?
- Are there overlaps, overflow, broken wrapping, or excessive reading?
- Do tabs, swipers, and horizontal labels support manual operation and scrolling back?
- Are controls and corresponding content visible together when practical?
- Are keyboard behavior, focus, semantic headings, landmarks, and reduced-motion preferences supported?

### Performance and delivery

- Are the first-screen message, CTA, and critical media available first?
- Do third-party scripts, fonts, or large animation dependencies block the first screen?
- Do forms, navigation, FAQ, carousels, and CTAs work in real interactions?
- Are images, social metadata, legal links, and SEO complete?
- Have relevant build, type, and quality checks been run?
- Have real desktop and mobile pages been checked rather than only the code?

A successful build proves engineering behavior, not better copy or design. Finally, verify the user's real task and conversion path.
