# Strategy and information architecture

For every mode, identify the page type and single primary conversion action first. Do not choose fonts, cards, or animation while the primary goal remains unclear.

## 1. Understand the context

Confirm or make reasonable assumptions about:

- Target audience, use case, and problem.
- Page type, traffic source, and visitor awareness.
- Primary conversion action and what counts as a successful conversion.
- The user's three main objections.
- Available product screenshots, cases, third-party validation, data, credentials, and risk reducers.

Page goals differ by type:

- **Landing page**: one audience, one core offer, one primary conversion action.
- **Homepage**: multiple audiences and secondary paths are allowed, but one primary CTA must remain clear.

Ask with a recommendation when a directional decision is unclear. Decide reversible local details directly.

## 2. Write the core message

Use one sentence to explain who the product is for, the situation in which they use it, how it works, and the result they receive.

Headlines state a conclusion or concrete difference. Supporting copy explains the mechanism, evidence, or limitations. CTA labels use a clear action and set an accurate expectation.

Avoid:

- Empty jargon such as empower, seamless, extreme, next generation, redefine, or unlock.
- Repeated "not X, but Y" phrasing used only for cadence.
- Eyebrows, subtitles, numbering, periods, or labels added only to balance a layout.
- Unsourced numbers, fake logo walls, fake testimonial portraits, and generated FAQ filler.
- Rephrasing the same claim across the hero, features, and final CTA.

Do not create choppy sentences, fake personality, excessive casualness, or a new formula merely to avoid AI slop.

## 3. Give every section one distinct job

Do not automatically apply Hero, Features, Pricing, and FAQ. Organize content around user decisions. Possible sections include:

- A real task and its outcome.
- The core product interface or workflow.
- Benefits, use cases, and limitations.
- Cases, third-party validation, data, and credentials.
- Delivery, trial conditions, and risk reduction.
- Genuine user questions.
- A final CTA consistent with the hero.

Every section must add new information. Merge or remove repeated sections instead of padding the page.

Use FAQ only for genuine questions, with no fixed item count. Put detailed content on secondary pages or in drawers and modals when appropriate.

## 4. Choose layouts by comprehension cost

- **Classic hero plus sections**: a real interface or demo explains the product well.
- **Long-form narrative**: the offer requires context, trust building, or objection handling.
- **Minimal conversion page**: visitors already have intent, or the offer is simple, such as a download, demo, or waitlist.
- **Comparison page**: visitors are evaluating alternatives or competitors.

These layouts are options, not templates. Follow the user's decision path when the content requires another structure.
