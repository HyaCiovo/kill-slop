# Evidence and credibility

## 1. Evidence priority

1. Real product screenshots, working demos, and real use-case imagery.
2. Verifiable customer cases and specific outcomes.
3. Verifiable third-party validation, such as independent reviews, attributed user testimonials, and traceable media coverage.
4. Data with a documented test method, testing reports, and credentials.
5. Clearly labeled sample data or guided demos.

Third-party validation must trace to a person, organization, or original source. Unverifiable logos, portraits, media names, and quotations are not proof.

Place evidence close to the claim it supports instead of burying it at the end of the page.

Real screenshots build trust. Decorative imagery supports pacing and tone. One cannot replace the other. When evidence is unavailable, narrow the claim or propose an evidence plan rather than inventing proof.

Label examples clearly. Visitors must not mistake a guided demo for a live business integration or real customer data.

## 2. Evidence gaps

During audits, list evidence gaps separately instead of writing only "needs social proof." For each gap, include:

- The claim or feature it affects.
- Existing content.
- Missing proof.
- Recommended material or verification method.
- How the page should limit its claim until evidence is available.

Do not replace specific numbers with vague adjectives on your own. Preserve user-provided numbers and attribution; mark missing sources and ask.

## 3. Media authenticity

- Product screenshots should come from a real version, with relevant dates or environments identified.
- Demo data must be labeled as an example and never presented as real business outcomes.
- Testimonials need an identifiable source and permission to use the attribution.
- Data needs a subject, time period, environment, and test method.
- Media coverage should link to the original source without misleading excerpts.
- Stock imagery must not imply customer relationships, product interfaces, or actual usage.
